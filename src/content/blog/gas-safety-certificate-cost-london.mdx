## What is a gas safety certificate?

A gas safety certificate — properly called a CP12, or Gas Safety Record — is a signed document confirming that a Gas Safe registered engineer has inspected every gas appliance, flue and pipe run in a rental property and found them safe to use. It lists each appliance checked and its result.

It's not optional paperwork. Under the **Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998**, every private landlord in England and Wales with a gas supply must have this inspection carried out every 12 months, without exception, for as long as the property is let.

## What actually moves the price

Three things determine what you pay:

**Number of appliances.** This is the main driver. A boiler alone is cheaper to inspect than a boiler plus a gas fire plus a hob, simply because each appliance needs its own set of checks — flame pattern, flue integrity, pressure, ventilation.

**Access and parking.** If an engineer has to pay for parking or can't park near the property, that cost is passed on as the flat £5 parking charge rather than buried in the headline price.

**Urgency.** Fixed-price providers like us don't charge more for booking quickly — we offer next-day appointments as standard. Where you'll see price spikes is with engineers who charge an "emergency call-out" premium for same-day work, which is one reason we'd always recommend booking your renewal before the current certificate actually expires.

## What happens during the inspection

A Gas Safe registered engineer visits the property and checks every gas appliance in turn — the boiler, hob, oven, and any gas fires — along with the pipework and the meter. For each one, they test for gas tightness, check ventilation is adequate, confirm the flue is sound, and verify the appliance is burning correctly. The whole visit typically takes 30–60 minutes for a standard flat.

You don't need to be present. A tenant, keyholder or letting agent can provide access, and we confirm the engineer's arrival window by text beforehand. The signed certificate is emailed to you within 24 hours of the visit.

## What happens if you don't have one

This is where the real cost of skipping a gas safety certificate shows up. Letting a property without a valid, in-date CP12 is a **criminal offence** under the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 — not a civil matter you can quietly fix later. The Health and Safety Executive and local councils both have the power to prosecute, and penalties include an **unlimited fine and up to two years' imprisonment**.

Beyond the legal exposure, a lapsed certificate also blocks you from serving a valid Section 21 notice, meaning you can't legally end a tenancy through the standard route until it's renewed. And if something does go wrong with a gas appliance at a property with no current inspection, you're personally exposed — insurers routinely decline claims where a landlord had no valid gas safety certificate in place.
