Becoming a **landlord** for the first time means taking on a series of legal obligations that did not exist when the property was your home. The compliance burden is not light — and the consequences of getting it wrong range from a £30,000 civil penalty to a criminal conviction. But the checklist itself is manageable if you know what you need.

This guide covers every certificate, check, and document a first-time landlord in England needs before renting out a property in 2026, along with the penalties for each one.

## Before You Rent: The Legal Minimum Checklist

| Certificate / Check | Frequency | Penalty for non-compliance |
|---|---|---|
| EICR (Electrical Safety) | Every 5 years | Up to £30,000 per breach |
| Gas Safety Certificate (CP12) | Every 12 months | Unlimited fine + imprisonment |
| EPC (Energy Performance) | Every 10 years (min. E rating) | Up to £5,000 |
| Smoke alarms | Check at start of each tenancy | Up to £5,000 |
| CO alarms | Check at start of each tenancy | Up to £5,000 |
| "How to Rent" guide | Each new tenancy | Affects possession rights |
| Deposit protection | Within 30 days of receipt | Up to 3× the deposit |

For HMO landlords, additional requirements apply: HMO licence, fire risk assessment, fire doors, and enhanced alarm systems. These are covered in the HMO section below.

## EICR — Who Needs One and When

An **Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR)** is mandatory for every privately rented property in England. Under the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020, landlords must:

- Have the fixed electrical installation inspected at least every five years
- Obtain an EICR from a qualified and competent electrician
- Provide a copy to new tenants before they move in
- Provide a copy to existing tenants within 28 days of the inspection

If the EICR is unsatisfactory (C1 or C2 defects found), you must arrange remedial work within 28 days. You cannot let the property to new tenants while the EICR is unsatisfactory.

**Who is exempt?** New build properties with a new electrical installation certificate (EIC) are exempt from the EICR requirement for the first 5 years.

**Cost:** [EICR certificates](/eicr) start from £94.99 for a 1–3 bedroom property. Studios from £67.99.

**Before booking:** Ensure access to the consumer unit (fuse box), all sockets, switches, and light fittings. Clear any stored items around the consumer unit.

## Gas Safety Certificate — Annual Requirement

A **Gas Safety Certificate (CP12)** is required every year for any property with gas appliances. The gas safety check must be carried out by a **Gas Safe registered engineer** — using an unregistered person is illegal.

Your obligations:
- Arrange an annual gas safety check for each property with gas
- Provide a copy of the current CP12 to new tenants **before they move in**
- Provide a copy to existing tenants within 28 days of each annual check
- Keep copies of the previous two years' certificates

**What if you miss the renewal?** There is no grace period. If your CP12 expires, you are immediately non-compliant. Book a renewal before the expiry date — the two-month early renewal rule (see the annual gas safety guide) allows you to renew up to two months early without losing your anniversary date.

**Cost:** [Gas safety certificates](/gas-safety-certificate) from £49.99 for 1 appliance, £59.99 for 2, £69.99 for 3.

## EPC — Minimum E Rating Required

An **Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)** tells tenants how energy-efficient the property is. Every privately rented property in England must have a valid EPC (within the last 10 years) rated at least E before it can be legally let.

Your obligations:
- Have a valid EPC in place before marketing the property
- Provide a copy to prospective tenants at the point of marketing
- Ensure the property is rated E or above
- Properties rated F or G cannot be legally let (subject to very limited exemptions)

**The 2030 minimum C proposal:** The government has proposed raising the minimum EPC rating for rental properties to C by 2030. This is not yet legislated but is widely expected to become law. Starting improvement works now — particularly loft insulation and boiler replacement — is advisable for properties currently rated D or E.

**Cost:** [EPC certificates](/epc) from £89.99 for a studio, £109.99 for 1–3 bedrooms.

## Fire Risk Assessment — When Required

A formal **fire risk assessment (FRA)** under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 is required for:

- **HMOs** — all houses in multiple occupation with communal areas
- **Blocks of flats** — communal areas (landlords of individual flats are responsible for communal areas if they are the freeholder)
- Any property with shared hallways, staircases, or communal facilities

For standard single-let houses and flats (rented to one household), a formal written FRA is not legally required — but smoke alarm compliance is.

**For HMO landlords:** A [fire risk assessment](/fire-risk-assessment) from a competent assessor is mandatory. The assessment must identify fire hazards, evaluate risks, and specify the required fire precautions including alarm systems, fire doors, and means of escape.

**Cost:** FRA from £74.99 for a studio, £139.99 for 1–3 bedrooms, up to £349.99 for an 8-bedroom HMO.

## Smoke and CO Alarms — What the Law Requires

The **Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarm (Amendment) Regulations 2022** require landlords to:

- Install at least one **smoke alarm on every storey** of the property (where there is a room used as living accommodation)
- Install a **carbon monoxide (CO) alarm** in any room with a gas, oil, or solid fuel burning appliance (including boiler rooms) — this was extended to gas boilers and fires in 2022
- **Test all alarms at the start of each tenancy** — typically by pressing the test button in the presence of the tenant at check-in
- **Repair or replace** any alarm that tenants report as faulty, as soon as reasonably practicable

Smoke alarms must be in working order — a flat battery or defective sensor is non-compliant. Keep a record of when you tested them and what the result was.

**Penalty:** Local authorities can impose a remedial notice and fine of up to £5,000 for non-compliance.

## PAT Testing — When Recommended

**Portable Appliance Testing (PAT)** is not a specific legal requirement under landlord legislation, but:

- The **Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations 1994** require any electrical equipment you supply to be safe
- The **Housing Act 2004** gives local authorities powers to act on electrical safety hazards in properties
- For **HMOs**, PAT testing of landlord-provided appliances is expected and is typically required by HMO licensing conditions

**Who should consider PAT testing:**
- Furnished lets — if you provide white goods, lamps, or kitchen appliances
- HMOs — every change of tenancy ideally
- Properties where appliances are more than 5 years old

**Cost:** [PAT testing](/pat-testing) from £59.99 for up to 10 appliances.

## HMO Licence Requirements If Applicable

A **House in Multiple Occupation (HMO)** is a property rented to three or more people from two or more separate households who share facilities such as a bathroom or kitchen.

**Mandatory HMO licensing** (national requirement) applies to HMOs with:
- Five or more occupants from two or more households
- Three or more storeys

**Additional HMO licensing** is applied by individual London boroughs and covers smaller HMOs. Most London boroughs have additional licensing schemes — check with your local council.

**Consequences of not licensing:**
- Unlimited fine
- Rent Repayment Order — tenants can claim back up to 12 months of rent
- Criminal record
- Ineligibility for certain possession grounds

## Serving Documents on Tenants Correctly

Several compliance documents must be **physically served** on tenants — simply having them is not enough:

| Document | When to serve |
|---|---|
| EPC | Before the tenancy begins (when marketing the property) |
| Gas Safety Certificate | Before move-in (new tenants) / within 28 days (renewals) |
| EICR | Before move-in (new tenants) / within 28 days (inspection) |
| "How to Rent" guide | At start of each new tenancy |
| Deposit protection info | Within 30 days of receiving the deposit |

Serve documents by email and keep a read-receipt or acknowledgement. For the "How to Rent" guide, serving the correct version is important — it is updated periodically, and you must serve the version current at the time the tenancy starts.

## Penalties Table — What Each Missing Certificate Can Cost

| Compliance failure | Penalty |
|---|---|
| No valid EICR | Civil penalty up to £30,000 per property |
| No valid Gas Safety Certificate | Unlimited fine + up to 6 months' imprisonment (criminal offence) |
| No valid EPC | Civil penalty up to £5,000 |
| No working smoke alarms | Civil penalty up to £5,000 |
| No CO alarm (where required) | Civil penalty up to £5,000 |
| Unlicensed HMO | Unlimited fine + Rent Repayment Order (up to 12 months' rent) |
| Missing deposit protection | Up to 3× deposit + cannot serve Section 21 |

These are maximum penalties. In practice, local authorities and the HSE take into account circumstances — but deliberate non-compliance and repeat offences are treated seriously.
