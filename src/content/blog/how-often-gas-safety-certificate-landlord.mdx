The **annual gas safety check** is one of the most time-critical compliance requirements for landlords. Unlike the EICR, which is valid for five years, or the EPC, which lasts ten years, the gas safety certificate (CP12) must be renewed **every 12 months** — and there is no grace period.

This guide explains exactly how the 12-month cycle works, when renewal falls due, how the two-month early renewal rule works, and what happens if you miss the date.

## The Annual Requirement — Where It Comes From

The legal requirement for annual gas safety checks is set out in the **Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998**, Regulation 36. The regulations impose a duty on landlords to:

- Ensure that each gas appliance, flue, and gas fitting they provide is maintained in a safe condition
- Have an annual inspection and safety check carried out on each appliance and flue
- Ensure the check is carried out by a **Gas Safe registered engineer**
- Keep a record of each check for at least two years
- Provide a copy of the current record to tenants as required

The "at least every 12 months" requirement is a minimum — not a maximum. There is nothing preventing you from having an additional check in between if you have concerns about an appliance, but the annual check is the legal baseline.

The Regulations were made under the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974. Breach is a criminal offence — not a civil matter with a financial penalty. This means prosecution by the Health and Safety Executive (HSE), which can result in an unlimited fine and up to six months' imprisonment.

## Exactly When the Renewal Falls Due

The CP12 is valid for 12 months from the **date of the inspection**. The certificate will state the date of the inspection and the date by which the next inspection is due — typically expressed as "this certificate expires on [date]" or "next inspection due by [date]".

The renewal must be carried out **on or before** the expiry date. There is no legal grace period.

**Example:** If the inspection was carried out on 15 March 2025, the certificate expires on 14 March 2026. The next inspection must be carried out by 14 March 2026. If you book it for 20 March 2026, you are in breach for the period 15–20 March.

This is why it is important to diarise renewal dates well in advance — ideally booking the next inspection when the current one is carried out.

## The Two-Month Early Renewal Rule

The Gas Safety Regulations include a helpful provision that allows landlords to renew the certificate early without losing their anniversary date. This is known informally as the "two-month rule".

Under Regulation 36(3)(e), if a gas safety check is carried out within the two months **before** the expiry date of the current certificate, the new certificate's expiry date is calculated as if the check had been carried out on the original expiry date — not on the actual date of the new inspection.

**Example:**
- Original certificate: inspection date 15 March 2024, expires 14 March 2025
- Early renewal: inspection carried out on 10 February 2025 (within two months before expiry)
- New certificate: counted as if carried out on 15 March 2025 → expires 14 March 2026

The result is that you have not shortened the validity period by renewing early. You can renew two months early, every year, and your renewal date stays on the same anniversary each year.

This is extremely useful for landlords who want to book the renewal before the busy season, or who want to avoid the period around a tenancy change when there may be access difficulties.

## What Happens If You Miss the Renewal Date

If the CP12 expires without a renewal inspection being carried out:

**You are immediately non-compliant.** There is no grace period. The moment the certificate expires, you are in breach of Regulation 36 of the Gas Safety Regulations.

**It is a criminal offence.** This is not a civil penalty — it is a criminal matter. The HSE can prosecute. In practice, the HSE typically focuses on cases where a gas incident has occurred or where non-compliance was persistent or deliberate, but the legal position is clear.

**Your tenant is entitled to be notified.** If the certificate expires while a tenancy is ongoing, you should inform your tenant and arrange the renewal urgently. Continuing to let the property without a valid CP12 is a continuing offence.

**What to do if you have missed the renewal:** Book an inspection immediately. The [gas safety certificate](/gas-safety-certificate) inspection will produce a new CP12 from the date of the new inspection — the anniversary date will shift to the new date (since you have not used the early renewal rule). Get the new certificate to your tenant as soon as it is issued.

## Moving Between Tenancies — What Is Required

When an existing tenancy ends and a new one begins:

**If the CP12 is still valid:** You do not need a new inspection solely because the tenancy has changed. However, you must provide a copy of the current CP12 to the new tenant **before they move in**. Do not wait until after move-in day to send it.

**If the CP12 expires around the time of the tenancy change:** Use the overlap to combine the end of one tenancy and the start of the next to book the annual renewal. Have the inspection carried out before the new tenant moves in, so the new CP12 can be provided to them on day one.

**If the CP12 has already expired at the point of tenancy change:** You cannot legally start the new tenancy without a valid CP12. Book the inspection immediately and do not allow the new tenant to move in until you can provide the certificate.

## New Properties — When the First Check Is Needed

For a newly built property:

- The gas installation should have been commissioned and certified by the installing contractor as part of the build
- The building regulations completion certificate covers the gas installation at the time of installation
- The first annual gas safety check under Regulation 36 is required within **12 months of the property first being let**

For landlords who purchase a new build property, the developer or managing agent should provide commissioning documentation. Check what documentation exists for the gas installation and diarise the first annual check date accordingly.

For properties that are converted (e.g., a commercial building converted to residential flats), the position is the same — the first annual check must occur within 12 months of the first tenancy.

## Commercial Properties — Same Annual Requirement

Commercial properties with gas installations require the commercial equivalent — a **CP42** (for commercial gas) rather than a CP12 (for domestic gas). The frequency requirement is the same: annual inspection by a Gas Safe registered engineer who is qualified to work on commercial gas systems.

Commercial gas safety records follow the same 12-month cycle and the same early renewal logic. If you are a landlord of mixed-use property with both residential and commercial tenants, you will need both a CP12 for the residential gas appliances and a CP42 for the commercial gas appliances.

## How to Keep Track of Renewals

For landlords with a single property, a calendar reminder works well. For landlords with a portfolio, a simple spreadsheet is essential:

| Property | Last inspection | Expiry date | Early renewal window opens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Main Street | 15 Mar 2025 | 14 Mar 2026 | 15 Jan 2026 |
| 5 High Road | 10 Jun 2025 | 9 Jun 2026 | 10 Apr 2026 |

Set calendar alerts for when the "early renewal window opens" (two months before expiry) so you can book the inspection before the busy period.

Many landlords instruct their letting agents to manage renewals. If you do this, ensure the agent has a reliable compliance tracking system and that you receive copies of all certificates — it is your legal responsibility, not the agent's.
